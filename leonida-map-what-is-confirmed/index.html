The GTA 6 map: Rockstar finally gave a number, and it still isn't one

Three times Red Dead Redemption 2 is the first official word on the map. The problem is that RDR2's size was never official either.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

For three years the most repeated fact about the Grand Theft Auto VI map was one Rockstar had never said. Roughly 125 square kilometres, two and a half times GTA V, six regions. It appeared everywhere, stated flatly, sourced to nothing.

Then on 27 August the studio finally spoke, and said the map is three times larger than Red Dead Redemption 2.

That's real, it's official, and it's the first hard statement about scale since the game was announced. It's also considerably less useful than it sounds.

The problem with the number

Rockstar has never published a map size for Red Dead Redemption 2.

So the one official statement about GTA 6's map is a multiple of a figure that was itself never confirmed. Three times an unknown is still an unknown. You can't do arithmetic with it, though a great many people spent the following forty-eight hours trying.

Every "GTA 6 is therefore X square kilometres" post published this week is a community estimate multiplied by three, presented with the confidence of a measurement. The multiplication is the easy part. The base number is the part nobody can source.

Relative size

Three times larger than Red Dead Redemption 2, stated by Rockstar during the Extended Look. Directional and official.

Any area figure

Both the old 125 km² estimate and every new figure derived from the RDR2 comparison. The base was never official.

What's actually confirmed about the place

The setting is the state of Leonida, a fictional Florida, with Vice City as its urban centre, modern day.

This is the series' third trip to Vice City by name — after the 2002 game and its 2006 handheld follow-up — but the first set now. The original was 1986, and the neon-and-pastel identity most of us carry around comes from that period rather than from the actual place.

Trailers and character material have established the Leonida Keys, where Jason lives; Ocean Beach, where the Vintage Vice City Pack garage sits; wetlands; backcountry; and towns well outside the metropolitan sprawl. Rockstar's own framing — the darkest side of the sunniest place in America — points straight at the gap between postcard Florida and everything behind it.

Why area was always a bad measurement

Even a confirmed square-kilometre figure wouldn't tell you much.

GTA V has a large map containing a substantial amount of empty mountain. Red Dead Redemption 2 has a bigger one with a completely different density of incident. And a Florida-shaped map contains an enormous amount of water — how that water is handled, as traversable space or decoration or loading screen, changes the practical size dramatically.

There's a detail from the Extended Look that says more than any area figure: airboating, kayaking, jetskiing, diving and swimming were all shown. That's five separate water activities. It suggests the wetlands aren't a border you drive past.

Square kilometres don't tell you how long the drive across town feels, and that's the only measurement anybody actually cares about.

The eighty-hour figure

Roughly eighty hours of story was mentioned alongside the map comparison.

Worth sitting with those two numbers together. Eighty hours across a map three times the size of Red Dead Redemption 2 implies eighty hours is what you get moving through it with blinkers on — the critical path, skipping the side content. Whether that's the intended reading, nobody has said.

The interior question, still open

Not how wide the map is. How much of it you can walk into.

GTA V had relatively few enterable interiors for its building count. Red Dead Redemption 2 pushed hard the other way, and its interiors did most of the work of making that world feel inhabited.

The Extended Look showed interiors — the opening drug pickup happens inside a building, and there was clubbing footage. But there's a difference between interiors that exist as mission spaces and interiors you can wander into on a Tuesday for no reason, and nothing shown settles which this is.

What would actually answer this

Three things, and none has happened:

  1. An uncut traversal shot. One continuous drive across the map, no cuts. Rockstar hasn't shown one.
  2. An official area figure, for this game or for Red Dead Redemption 2 retroactively.
  3. The review embargo. Reviewers with a finished build and a word count are the first people who can

answer density questions honestly. Most map speculation dies there.

Until then: the state is Leonida, the city is Vice City, the era is now, it's three times Red Dead Redemption 2, and nobody outside Rockstar knows what that means in kilometres.
