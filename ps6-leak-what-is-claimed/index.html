The PS6 leak, sorted: what's claimed, who's claiming it, and what Sony has actually said

A leaker says the PS6 chip is finished, rated at 40 teraflops, for a November 2027 launch. Sony hasn't said a word. Every claim, sorted by what backs it.

Unverified. Treat as speculation until an official source says otherwise.

Over the past week, one hardware leaker's posts have turned into headlines about the PlayStation 6: finished chip, specs, a launch month, even a price. It's being repeated as if it were a spec sheet.

It isn't. Sony has not announced the PS6, its specs, its date, or its price. This post carries a Rumour stamp, and here's each claim sorted by what's behind it.

Where it comes from

Almost everything traces back to KeplerL2, a leaker who posts on the NeoGAF forum and whose track record outlets take seriously. Outlets including Insider Gaming, VideoCardz, and Push Square reported the posts from 23 September onward. Insider Gaming and Push Square both stress that none of this is official.

The claims

The chip is finished

Kepler says the PS6's chip has "taped out," meaning the design is complete and has gone to manufacturing. The same claim is made for the next Xbox, referred to in the leak as Helix.

40 teraflops

Kepler puts the PS6 at 40 teraflops of compute, roughly double the PS5, and the next Xbox at 56. Teraflops are a rough measure: the PS5 was rated lower than the Xbox Series X and held up fine, so a paper gap doesn't settle which machine performs better.

November 2027

A launch month attributed to the same leaker. Nothing from Sony supports or contradicts it.

A handheld and a $900 price

Push Square's report of the leak includes a portable model and a manufacturing cost of around $960 with a possible $900 retail price. These are the least supported claims in the set, and price is the kind of detail that is usually decided close to launch.

The disc cut-off

In July, PlayStation announced it will stop producing physical discs for new games from January 2028. Existing physical games keep working.

If a November 2027 launch were true, the PS6 would arrive two months before that switch. That timing is interesting, but it's our observation, not evidence. The disc decision stands on its own either way.

How to read this

A finished chip, if true, means the hardware exists in final form. It says nothing about when Sony will announce it, what it will cost after this year's console price rises and the memory shortage, or whether a handheld ships alongside it.

Treat the teraflop numbers as plausible, the date as possible, and the price as a guess until Sony speaks. We'll update this when it does.
