GTA 6 was built without generative AI — while the rest of the industry bets the other way

Rockstar confirmed no generative AI in development and no single-player microtransactions — a deliberate bet against where the industry is heading.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

The biggest game of the decade was made by hand. That's now confirmed, and it's a more interesting fact than it first sounds — because almost everyone else in the industry is going the other way.

Rockstar North co-studio head Rob Nelson told Kinda Funny Games, during a preview visit to the Edinburgh studio, that generative AI played no part in building Grand Theft Auto VI, and that the single-player game has no microtransactions. Both answers were blunt one-word "no"s, which is about as unambiguous as Rockstar gets.

What was actually said

No generative AI in development

Asked directly whether generative AI was used to build the game, Nelson said no. This matches what Take-Two chairman Strauss Zelnick told GamesIndustry in February — that generative AI has no part in what Rockstar is building, and its worlds are constructed street by street rather than generated.

No single-player microtransactions

Nelson confirmed there is no way to spend real money inside the single-player game launching 19 November.

The New York Times, in a feature from the same preview week, backed up the human-made framing with a number that does the arguing for it: Grand Theft Auto VI reportedly contains over 600,000 animations. For scale, GTA V had roughly 55,000 and Red Dead Redemption 2 around 300,000. Whatever else that number means, it's not the signature of a studio cutting corners with generated content.

The distinction that matters

"No AI in the game" is being repeated everywhere, and it's slightly wrong in a way worth correcting.

What Rockstar ruled out is generative AI — the kind that produces new art, text, or audio from learned patterns. The traditional AI that has run Grand Theft Auto for years — the systems governing traffic, pedestrians, and police behaviour — is still very much there. That's AI in the classic sense, and it always has been. It's the newer, controversial kind that Nelson said no to.

The difference isn't pedantry. A game with no NPC behaviour AI would be a ghost town. A game with no generative AI is one where humans made every asset. Those are opposite statements, and only the second is what Rockstar claimed.

Why it's a genuine stance, not just a fact

Here's what makes this a story rather than a footnote: Rockstar is betting against the direction most of the industry is moving.

The counter-examples piled up over the past year. Activision kept AI-generated assets in Call of Duty: Black Ops 7 even after players spotted them. Ubisoft admitted AI-made art had slipped into Anno 117: Pax Romana and pulled it after backlash. Electronic Arts has publicly demonstrated its own AI tools for building game environments. The 2026 State of the Game Industry survey found 52% of developers now think generative AI is bad for the industry — up from 30% a year earlier — even as studios adopt it.

Against that, Rockstar joins a smaller camp. CD Projekt Red, maker of The Witcher, has said its next game will avoid generative AI too. So the biggest release in the medium is landing firmly on the "made by people" side of the industry's sharpest current argument.

Whether that's principle or pragmatism is a fair question. Rockstar doesn't need to cut costs with AI the way a struggling studio might, and avoiding the technology also removes a pre-launch controversy from a game that already generates enough of them. A cynic and an idealist can both explain this decision. What's not in doubt is which side of the line the game landed on.

The part nobody should oversell

Two caveats, because this is exactly the kind of good-news story that gets stretched.

The no-microtransactions confirmation covers single-player only. Rockstar declined to discuss the next version of GTA Online when Kinda Funny raised it, and GTA Online has sold Shark Cards — real-money currency packs — for over a decade alongside GTA V. Nothing has been said about how the GTA VI online component will be monetised, and it would be naive to assume the answer is "not at all."

And "no generative AI in development" is a statement about the past, made by one executive in an interview. It's credible and it's corroborated, but it isn't a technical audit. Take it as what it is: a confirmed statement of intent from the studio, carrying the Confirmed stamp because named Rockstar leadership said it on the record — not as an independently verified fact about every pipeline in a multi-year project.

For a game this size, though, "we made it ourselves" is a real position to take. Most of the industry wouldn't, or couldn't, say the same right now.
