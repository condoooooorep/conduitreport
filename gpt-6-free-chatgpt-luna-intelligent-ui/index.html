GPT-6 is now in free ChatGPT. Here's which version you get, and what Intelligent UI does

Free ChatGPT users now get GPT-6 Luna and paid plans get Sol, with answers that can include charts, buttons and forms. What changed and who gets what.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

If you use ChatGPT, the model answering you has probably changed this month. On 7 October OpenAI rolled out GPT-6 to everyone in ChatGPT, free accounts included. Until then, GPT-6 had only been available on paid plans and to developers.

Which GPT-6 you get depends on your plan, and the answers themselves can now look quite different.

What's confirmed

Free and Go plans

GPT-6 Luna, the smaller and cheaper of the two models. Rollout to these plans began on 8 October, the day after the announcement.

Paid plans

GPT-6 Sol for Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise, from 7 October. Enterprise access depends on each company's admin settings.

What it replaces

Sol and Luna replace the GPT-5.6 versions of both models in ChatGPT, according to OpenAI's system card.

Chat only

The change covers ChatGPT's chat. The models behind ChatGPT's Work mode and Codex, OpenAI's coding tool, haven't changed.

OpenAI's announcement doesn't give usage limits for either plan.

What Intelligent UI means

The visible change is what OpenAI calls Intelligent UI. GPT-6 in ChatGPT is trained to "compose responses using text, visuals and interactive elements," choosing the format to fit the question. In practice that means an answer can include charts, buttons you can tap, forms to fill in, and small tools that work inside the conversation, not just paragraphs and lists.

Ask for a comparison and you might get a chart. Ask to plan something and you might get a form. It's the model deciding the layout, so the same question won't always come back in the same shape.

What OpenAI says changed under the hood

OpenAI's system card compares the new models with the GPT-5.6 versions they replace:

Factuality

OpenAI reports "significant improvements in factuality" across nearly all its tests, with one exception: errors in individual claims on prompts that depend heavily on facts.

Jailbreaks

Stronger resistance to attempts to trick the model past its rules, including ones that build up over several messages.

Mixed safety results

Some of OpenAI's content-safety tests got worse, with both models regressing in some categories. OpenAI says the failures it reviewed were "borderline but still generally safe," and that both models meet its safety bar.

Risk rating

OpenAI treats both as "High capability" in cybersecurity and in biological and chemical risks under its Preparedness Framework, which means extra safeguards apply.

All of these results come from OpenAI's own testing.

Where this fits

GPT-6 arrived in stages. GPT-6 Astra, the top model, launched on 3 September. Sol and Luna followed on 22 September, at about half the price of the models they replaced. This is the step that puts GPT-6 in front of most ChatGPT users.

The practical upshot: if you're on the free plan, you're now using a newer model than you were a month ago, and the gap between free and paid is the gap between Luna and Sol, not between generations.
