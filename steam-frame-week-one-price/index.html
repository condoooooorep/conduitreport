Steam Frame, one week in: the best wireless PC VR headset, priced by the memory shortage

Valve's $1,059 headset is shipping to its first buyers. Reviewers love the streaming and comfort, and keep coming back to the price. What's confirmed.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

Valve's Steam Frame has been on sale for just over a week, the purchase lottery has run, and the first buyers are checking out. With reviews now in, the picture is consistent: it's the best wireless PC VR experience available, and it costs more than Valve wanted it to — for a reason that has nothing to do with VR.

What's confirmed

Price and availability

Valve opened Steam Frame reservations on 14 September at $1,059 (256GB) and $1,299 (1TB). Sign-ups closed 17 September, the list was randomised, and purchase emails began going out on 18 September with a 72-hour window to check out. One sign-up per household, and your Steam account needed a purchase made before 27 April 2026.

The hardware

Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 running SteamOS, 16GB of RAM, dual 2160 × 2160 LCD panels at 72–144Hz (144Hz listed as experimental), a rear-mounted battery, and a Wi-Fi 6E adapter in the box for streaming from a PC. Half-Life: Alyx is included. Valve counts over 100 games Standalone Verified at launch.

The accessories are where the price creeps. Valve's 45W power supply is $29 and sold separately, and the Ergonomic Accessories Kit — the one with the top strap most reviewers say you'll want — is another $59. Realistically, a comfortable setup lands closer to $1,150.

The randomised lottery is the same approach Valve used for the Steam Machine in June, and the stated reason is the same: stop resellers buying up launch stock. Whether it worked will show in how much resale listings command over the next few weeks.

What reviewers agree on

The verdict

Reviews are broadly positive on the core experience and conflicted on value. Wireless PC streaming is the standout — PC Gamer reported a seamless connection during home and office testing — and IGN and PC Gamer both called it among the most comfortable headsets they've used, helped by moving the battery to the back of the strap.

The criticisms repeat across outlets: battery life of roughly 90 minutes to two hours in general use, and less in demanding games; passthrough that trails the Quest 3; speakers; and inconsistent compatibility for games running through emulation on the ARM chip. Several reviewers also noted launch software bugs of the kind Valve usually patches over the following months.

The comparison everyone reaches for is Meta's Quest 3, which costs roughly half as much. The Frame wins on RAM, refresh rate, and the quality of PC streaming. It loses on price, passthrough, and battery. For someone already deep in SteamVR, that trade makes sense. For anyone else, the Quest is the easier recommendation.

The real story is the price

Valve reportedly aimed to sell the Frame for less than the Index's $999 full kit. It didn't manage it, and the reason is the same one that has been pushing up the cost of every device with a memory chip in it this year.

The Steam Machine went through exactly this in June, launching at $1,049 — well above expectations — with Valve pointing at DRAM and storage costs. The Frame's 16GB of LPDDR5X and its 256GB or 1TB of storage sit squarely in the parts of the market being squeezed by AI data centres buying up supply. We've covered how that shortage works; the Steam Frame is what it looks like when it lands on a specific product you were about to buy.

Valve doesn't subsidise hardware the way Meta does, so it can't absorb the increase and make it back on software. What the parts cost is, more or less, what you pay.

Who it's for

Buy it if you already own a capable gaming PC, play a lot of SteamVR, and want to do it wirelessly from the sofa rather than a cleared-out room with base stations. The streaming is the best available.

Wait if you want mixed reality, long standalone sessions, or a first VR headset. The Quest 3 does more of that for much less, and the Frame's software is still settling.

Skip the reseller market entirely. If you missed the lottery, Valve's next wave is the way in — paying a scalper a premium on a headset already priced up by the memory crisis is the worst version of this.
