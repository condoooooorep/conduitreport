Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 verdict: an 84 so far, and a stronger Steam start than Black Ops 7

Modern Warfare 4 launches today with an 84 on Metacritic's PC page and 94% positive on Steam. What critics say about the campaign, and what isn't reviewed yet.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 launches today, 23 October, on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, PC and Nintendo Switch 2. Early reviews are good, and players who pre-ordered are even more positive.

One thing to know before reading any score: so far, critics have reviewed the campaign only. Multiplayer and DMZ weren't available to review before launch.

What's confirmed

The score so far

84 on Metacritic's PC page, from 11 critic reviews, all of them positive. The PS5 and Switch 2 pages don't have enough reviews yet for a score.

Player reviews

94% positive on Steam, rated Very Positive, from players who got the campaign early through pre-orders, as GamesRadar+ reported. GamesRadar+ says that's well ahead of both Black Ops 7 and Modern Warfare 3.

Release

23 October, $69.99, from Infinity Ward with Digital Legends Entertainment, as Nintendo Life lists it.

What critics praised

The campaign feels like it matters again. IGN gave it 8 out of 10 and said "Modern Warfare 4 made me feel something for the series again." GameSpot gave it 7 and called it "another edge-of-the-seat thrill ride." PC Gamer said the campaign doesn't feel like an afterthought to Warzone and Zombies, and GamingTrend called the mission flow fluid, as Pure Xbox's roundup collects.

Insider Gaming went furthest, with a 9 and a verdict calling it "the greatest Call of Duty campaign ever made," according to Nintendo Life's roundup.

What they didn't

The structure. GamesRadar+ said "rigid mission design and frustrating restrictions threaten to spoil the fun." GamesBeat found the open-world sections a poor fit for the story, slowing the pace. And IGN, despite its 8, doesn't think the campaign quite reaches the series' very best.

What's still to come

Multiplayer and DMZ. For many Call of Duty players they're the main event, and none of the reviews so far cover them. The Metacritic score can move as full reviews arrive, and we'll add a note when they do.

The Switch 2 version. As of 18 October, Nintendo Life had published impressions, with its full review still to come. How well a Call of Duty runs on Nintendo's hardware is one of the more interesting questions of this launch.

Should you buy it now

If you play for the campaign: the reviews are in, and they're good. It's a safe buy.

If you play for multiplayer: waiting a week costs you nothing but some early levels, and you'll know whether the online side holds up.

It lands four weeks before GTA 6, one of the last big releases before November, as our autumn release rundown tracks.

Update, 24 October

Full reviews covering multiplayer and DMZ are now arriving. Destructoid gave it 90, Game Informer 8.5 and The Guardian 80, and OpenCritic showed a 77 average from nine reviews with every critic recommending it, as GameDaily's roundup collected on 23 October. GameSpot praised the multiplayer and DMZ; Game Informer found multiplayer can get repetitive; Press Start said "strict matchmaking means sweaty lobbies and lengthy queues." Early player feedback on multiplayer is rougher than on the campaign, with complaints about maps, spawns and skill-based matchmaking, according to Push Square.
