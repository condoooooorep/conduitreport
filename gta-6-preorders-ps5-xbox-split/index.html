Are GTA 6 pre-orders really "heavily skewed" to PS5? The Verge says yes, Xbox says no

The Verge reported GTA 6 pre-orders are heavily skewed toward PS5. Xbox's strategy chief says its share matches its share of the console market. Who said what.

Carried by established outlets, not officially confirmed.

Seven weeks before launch, Grand Theft Auto VI has become a proxy fight about the health of Xbox.

On 24 September, The Verge's Tom Warren reported that GTA 6 pre-orders were "heavily skewed" toward PS5, citing sources at Xbox, and said it was "ringing yet more alarm bells about the platform." Within hours, Xbox's Chief Strategy Officer, Matthew Ball, replied publicly and disputed it.

No one has published an actual number. That's the most important thing to know before reading anything else about this.

Who said what

The Verge's report

Pre-orders for GTA 6 are "heavily skewed" toward PS5, according to sources at Xbox. The report framed it as another warning sign for Xbox, coming the same week as its latest round of layoffs and studio restructuring.

Xbox's response

Matthew Ball, Xbox's Chief Strategy Officer, replied directly: "Tom, we are very happy with GTA VI pre-orders. The title is breaking records for XBOX, we expect even more records will be broken throughout the fall, and our internal analysis shows our share of pre-orders to be matching our share of the console market." He also called parts of the report false.

Both statements can be true

Read carefully, the two claims don't contradict each other as much as the headlines suggest.

PS5 has sold far more consoles than Xbox Series X|S this generation. If Xbox's share of GTA 6 pre-orders simply matches its share of the console market, pre-orders will still lean heavily toward PS5 — because the install base does. Push Square made the same point: a split of roughly three PS5 pre-orders for every Xbox one would be the expected baseline, not a crisis.

So "heavily skewed to PS5" and "matching our market share" can describe the same numbers. The real question is whether Xbox is underperforming its own share, and neither side has shown data that answers it.

Why this story exists at all

Context matters. This lands in the same week Microsoft cut more jobs and moved several studios under Activision, which we covered in the Xbox layoffs and Undead Labs restructuring. It also follows this year's console price rises. Any number about Xbox right now gets read as a verdict on its future.

It's also worth noting what "breaking records for Xbox" does and doesn't mean. A game this anticipated would be expected to set pre-order records on every platform it's on, so the phrase says more about GTA 6 than about Xbox's position.

Our read

Treat "heavily skewed" as a reported claim from unnamed sources, and "matching our share" as a company statement with no figures attached. Neither is a sales number. The first real data will come from Take-Two's earnings, and from launch-week charts after 19 November.
