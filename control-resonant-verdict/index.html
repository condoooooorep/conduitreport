Control Resonant's verdict: an 84, Remedy's gamble paid off — mostly

Control Resonant scored 84 on Metacritic and OpenCritic, above the original. Critics love the world and combat, with one recurring complaint. The verdict.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

Control Resonant launched on 24 September, and the verdict is in: an 84 on both Metacritic and OpenCritic. Remedy changed the protagonist, the setting, and the combat all at once — and critics mostly agree the gamble paid off.

What's confirmed

The scores

Metacritic 84 from around 60 reviews, with only one negative. OpenCritic 84, in its "Mighty" tier, with most critics recommending it. That's above the original Control, which scored 82 in 2019.

Release details

Out now on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, and PC, with Mac later in 2026. The standard edition is $59.99; the $69.99 Digital Deluxe added two days of early access from 22 September, an art book, and the soundtrack. Physical copies arrive on 15 October.

The range

The average hides a wide spread. VGC gave it a perfect 10, GameSpot a 9, and IGN an 8. Eurogamer and GamesRadar+ both gave it 3 out of 5.

What reviewers loved: the world and the movement. The warped Manhattan setting, the art direction, and a new Shift ability that lets Dylan switch which way gravity pulls him drew consistent praise. The combat centres on the Aberrant, a shape-shifting weapon that changes form mid-fight, and several outlets called the combat design excellent.

The recurring complaint: combat thins out. Several reviews — including some positive ones — say it starts to feel repetitive in the back half of the campaign. The lower scores also argue that leaving the Oldest House cost the sequel some of what made the original feel so strange and specific.

The comparison worth making

Put this next to Marvel's Wolverine, which landed at 78 earlier this month. Wolverine was the bigger-budget, safer-seeming bet from a studio with a strong track record, and it came in below expectations. Control Resonant was the riskier sequel — new lead, new genre lean, new setting — from a smaller studio, and it came in above its predecessor, at $10 less.

It's not Alan Wake 2-level acclaim. But for a sequel that changed this much, an 84 with near-universal recommendation is a strong result.

Should you buy it

Yes, if you liked the original Control or Alan Wake 2, or you want a strange, stylish action game with a world that's worth exploring for its own sake. At $59.99, it's also cheaper than most big releases this autumn.

Maybe wait, if repetitive combat in long campaigns wears on you — that's the one complaint that comes up across reviews of every score.
