The Blood of Dawnwalker: the vampire RPG that quietly opened this brutal month

The dark-fantasy RPG where you're part human, part vampire, and the clock is always running. What it is, and why it's worth attention in a crowded September.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

Before the big exclusives and the twenty-year revivals, September opened on 3 September with The Blood of Dawnwalker — a dark-fantasy RPG that's the most interesting new idea of the month, and the one most at risk of being buried by everything landing around it.

What it is

The release

Out now on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, and PC. A single-player action RPG set in a 14th-century Europe ravaged by the Black Death and a vampire uprising.

The hook is the premise. You play a character caught between two states — part human, part vampire — and the game is built around that duality. As a human you move through the world by day; as a vampire you gain power but operate under different rules. The studio has built the game around a time pressure: the story runs on a clock, and you can't do everything, so how you spend your days and nights genuinely shapes which threads you resolve.

That's a real design idea rather than a cosmetic one. Most RPGs let you eventually do everything if you grind long enough. Dawnwalker forces choices by making time itself the scarce resource, which means two players can come out of it having seen genuinely different games.

Why it's worth attention

The vampire angle is well-worn; the dual-existence-under-a-clock structure is not. It's the kind of mechanic that either becomes frustrating — locking you out of content you wanted — or becomes the thing that makes the game memorable, depending on execution. Early reception has leaned positive, which suggests it lands closer to the second.

It also comes from the pedigree of people who've worked on large narrative RPGs, and it shows in the ambition: a reactive world, meaningful choices, and a setting — plague-era Europe with a supernatural overlay — that games rarely attempt.

The catch

Timing. Opening a month that also contains Onimusha's return, NBA 2K27, Marvel's Wolverine, Control, Silent Hill, and the long shadow of GTA 6 means Dawnwalker had roughly a day in the spotlight before the next release arrived. That's a shame for a game trying something genuinely different rather than iterating a known formula.

If the premise appeals — a choice-driven RPG where time is the enemy and you're on both sides of the human/monster line — it's worth not letting it vanish under the September pile-up.
