Gemini 4 Argon is here, but cyber defenders get it first

Google's new frontier model, Gemini 4 Argon, is priced from $2/$10 per million tokens. It goes to vetted cyber defenders first. What's confirmed.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

Six days after DeepMind's new head said Gemini 4 would come "as soon as possible," Google announced it. Gemini 4 Argon went official on 30 September, and Google calls it a new "frontier model."

The speed fits what Koray Kavukcuoglu said on stage at The Information's AI Agenda Live summit on 24 September, his first public appearance since taking over Google DeepMind in August: that Gemini 4 was in post-training, was already running internally to power Google's Antigravity coding tool, and that he hoped to release it "much earlier" than the end of the year.

There's a catch. You can't use it yet unless you're one of a small group of cybersecurity defenders. Everything below is from Google's own announcement.

What's confirmed

What it's for

Long, complex professional work: software engineering, enterprise knowledge work, and cybersecurity defence. Google says it's "built to sustain deep reasoning across complex, long-horizon workflows."

The benchmarks

77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1, a test of real-world software engineering; first on AutomationBench at 51.3%; 91.7% on LVBench for long video understanding; and 68% on CWE-bench for fixing security vulnerabilities, tied for first. Google also claims it leads the Vals Index across finance, coding, legal, and tax work.

Output limit

Google has raised the model's output limit to 1 million tokens, up from 64,000, so it can produce hundreds of thousands of tokens of work in a single run.

Price

$2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens at launch, rising to $4 and $20 after an introductory period. Cached input is 95% cheaper.

Who actually gets it

Defenders first

Argon is rolling out first through Google's Fairwind programme to trusted cyber defenders, who get it "without cyber guardrails" for full capability. Google says it will reach developers, enterprises, and consumers "as soon as possible," through the paid API and Google AI Ultra subscriptions.

There's no public date. That's the same approach the big labs took with their most capable security models earlier this month, which we covered in the cyber-AI arms race: the more a model can find and fix vulnerabilities, the more carefully it's released.

How it compares

Google says Argon beats OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra and Anthropic's latest models on several benchmarks. Those are Google's own comparisons, on tests Google chose. Independent results will come once more people can use it.

The timing is pointed. It lands a day after OpenAI's DevDay, where GPT-6.1 Sol offered near-top performance at a fifth of the price. Argon's introductory price of $2 and $10 per million tokens puts it right in that fight.

Our read

Gemini 4 is real, and on Google's numbers it's at or near the top. For most people, though, nothing changes today. The release to cyber defenders first is the story: Google is treating its best model's security skills as something to hand out carefully. We'll update this when it reaches the API and AI Ultra.
