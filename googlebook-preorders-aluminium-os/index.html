Googlebook is here: Google's Android laptops go on pre-order from $899

Five PC makers, Google's new Aluminium OS, and a choice of Intel or Snapdragon chips. Pre-orders are open and machines ship in early October.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

Google opened pre-orders for Googlebook on 21 September — its new premium laptop platform, built with five PC makers and running a new operating system. It's Google's most serious attempt yet to compete with the MacBook and Windows at the higher end of the laptop market, rather than the budget end Chromebooks lived in.

What's confirmed

The launch

Pre-orders opened 21 September, starting at $899, with machines from Acer, Lenovo, ASUS, HP, and Dell. Google says they begin shipping in early October in the US, Canada, the UK, Ireland, France, Germany, and Australia.

The software

Googlebooks run Aluminium OS, Google's new Android-based laptop operating system announced in May, which merges Android and ChromeOS, with Gemini built in throughout.

The chips split between two camps. Three launch models — from Acer, Lenovo, and ASUS — use Intel's new Panther Lake processors (Core Ultra Series 3). The other two use Qualcomm's Snapdragon X Elite.

Ship date

One outlet cites 4 October as the specific ship date. Google itself has only said "early October."

What makes it different from a Chromebook

Chromebooks were built around the browser and priced to be cheap — they won schools, not professionals. A Googlebook starts at $899, runs Android apps natively on a desktop-class OS, and is pitched squarely at the premium buyer. Google is effectively rebranding and relaunching its laptop ambitions, and trying to leave the "cheap school laptop" image behind.

It's also an AI-first pitch. Gemini is woven through the OS, which is the same bet Microsoft made with Copilot+ PCs and Apple made with Apple Intelligence.

The competition

The timing puts Googlebook in an unusual three-way fight:

  • Apple has the MacBook Neo at $599, its cheaper laptop built on an iPhone chip — undercutting Googlebook

on price.

  • Microsoft's Windows partners have Copilot+ PCs, and many of the same five makers build them.
  • Nvidia is preparing its RTX Spark PC chip for Windows laptops this autumn.

For Intel, landing three of the five launch designs is a genuine win. For Qualcomm, Googlebook gives its laptop chips a second home beyond Windows.

Should you pre-order?

Probably not yet. It's a new operating system on day one, and first-generation platforms tend to arrive with rough edges and thin software support. The question worth waiting to see answered in reviews is how well Android apps work as full desktop apps — that decides whether this is a real alternative to a Mac or Windows laptop, or an expensive Chromebook. At $899 and up, the reviews are worth the wait.
