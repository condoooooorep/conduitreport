How big is the GTA 6 map? Everything Rockstar has confirmed about Leonida

Rockstar says the GTA 6 map is about twice GTA V's and three times Red Dead Redemption 2's, with six regions. What's confirmed, and what no figure tells you.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

For three years the most repeated fact about the Grand Theft Auto VI map was one Rockstar had never said: roughly 125 square kilometres, two and a half times GTA V. It appeared everywhere, stated flatly, sourced to nothing.

Rockstar has now spoken about the map's size several times, and two senior developers have given the same answer. This page brings together everything it has confirmed about Leonida, and what those numbers can and can't tell you.

How big it is

About twice GTA V

Rockstar North co-studio head Rob Nelson told press in August that the map is about twice the size of GTA V's, with Vice City itself about twice the size of Los Santos.

Three times Red Dead Redemption 2

Rockstar told The New York Times the same week that the map is three times the playable area of Red Dead Redemption 2.

Rockstar's biggest "by some degree"

In Game Informer's September cover story, Rockstar's Aaron Garbut repeated the twice-GTA-V comparison and called it the biggest map the studio has built "by some degree," as VGC reported.

Any square-kilometre figure

Every area figure in circulation, old or new, is a fan estimate. Rockstar has never published one.

The catch with both comparisons

Rockstar has never published a map size for GTA V or for Red Dead Redemption 2.

So the official answers are multiples of figures that were themselves never official. "Twice GTA V" is clear as a comparison, and it's the useful one: most players know how big Los Santos and Blaine County feel. But you can't turn it into kilometres without starting from a fan measurement.

That's why every "GTA 6 is X square kilometres" headline is an estimate multiplied out. It might be close. It still isn't a figure Rockstar has given.

Where it is

The setting

The state of Leonida, a fictional Florida, set in the present day, with Vice City as its biggest city.

Six regions

Vice City, the Leonida Keys, Grassrivers, Port Gellhorn, Ambrosia, and Mount Kalaga National Park, from Game Informer's cover story.

This is the series' third trip to Vice City by name, after the 2002 game and its 2006 handheld follow-up, but the first set in the present. The originals were set in the 1980s, and the neon-and-pastel look most people picture comes from that era rather than the real place.

Trailers and character material have also shown the Leonida Keys, where Jason lives; Ocean Beach; wetlands; backcountry; and small towns well outside the city.

What size doesn't tell you

Even an official square-kilometre figure wouldn't say much about how the world plays.

GTA V has a large map with a lot of empty mountain. Red Dead Redemption 2 has a bigger one with a very different amount going on in it. And a Florida-shaped map contains a great deal of water. Whether that water is somewhere you spend time or just scenery changes the practical size enormously. The Extended Look showed airboating, kayaking, jet-skiing, diving and swimming, which suggests the wetlands are meant to be used, not driven past.

The better question has always been density: how much of the map you can go into.

Denser, with far more interiors

Garbut told Game Informer the map is denser and more detailed than GTA V's, with far more interiors: "We obsessed about every part – the rooftops, the alleys, the internals and everything in between."

That's the most important thing Rockstar has said about the map. GTA V had relatively few buildings you could enter. Red Dead Redemption 2 went the other way, and its interiors did a lot of the work of making that world feel lived in. Garbut's comments suggest GTA 6 leans the Red Dead way. What it hasn't shown is how many of those interiors you can wander into freely, rather than only during missions.

How long it takes to play

Rob Nelson said his own playthrough took about 80 hours, and that it included some optional objectives as well as the main story. That's one developer's run, not an official length, and it says little about how long the whole map takes to explore.

What would settle it

Two things would answer what the numbers can't:

  1. An uncut drive across the map. One continuous shot, no edits. Rockstar hasn't shown one, including

in the launch trailer.

  1. The reviews. Critics with the finished game are the first people who can say how full the map really

is. Reports say reviews will land before launch.

Until then, what's confirmed is this: Leonida is about twice the size of GTA V's map, three times Red Dead Redemption 2's, split into six regions, and, by Rockstar's account, much denser than either. The game is out on 19 November on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S.
