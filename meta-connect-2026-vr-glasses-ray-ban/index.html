Meta Connect 2026: $1,300 VR Glasses, new Ray-Bans, and the Display goes international

Meta's 100-gram VR Glasses cost $1,300 and ship spring 2027. Ray-Ban Meta Gen 3 is $449 and out now. Everything confirmed at Connect 2026, with dates.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

Meta used Connect on 23 September to put a new, premium tier above its Quest headsets. It calls the product VR Glasses: a 100-gram visor with a tethered compute puck, a $1,300 price, and a spring 2027 ship date.

Everything below is from Meta's own announcements.

Meta VR Glasses

Price and date

$1,300 ($1,299.99) with 128GB of storage, shipping in spring 2027. Controllers are optional and sold separately or in bundles.

The hardware

Around 100 grams on your face, with magnesium alloy frames and pancake lenses. Dual 120Hz HDR micro-OLED displays at 2412×2288 per eye, a 70-by-66-degree field of view, and full-colour passthrough. Eye, hand and face tracking are built in.

The puck

The processing lives in a 300-gram puck running Qualcomm's Snapdragon Reality Elite with 12GB of RAM, connected by a hybrid fibre-optic cable. Battery life is up to about three hours.

The weight is the point. Taking the chip and battery off your head is how Meta gets a headset down to glasses territory, and the trade-off is a cable to a box in your pocket. Apple's Vision Pro kept the computer on your head, and paid for it in weight.

Meta says Quest 3 apps will run on it at higher resolution using foveated rendering, and around 75 games will support hand gestures at launch. It's also the first VR product with IMAX Enhanced certification, and USB-C DisplayPort input lets you use it as a screen for a phone, laptop, or handheld.

The glasses you can buy now, or soon

Ray-Ban Meta Gen 3

From $449, on sale from 23 September. A 12MP camera with 3K video, a six-microphone array, and up to nine hours of battery.

Ray-Ban Meta Audio

From $349, out 13 October. No camera at all: audio-only glasses at 43 grams with 12-hour battery life, and prescription lens support.

Meta Adventurer

Meta's own-brand glasses, from $249. Pre-orders opened on 23 September, shipping 23 October.

Meta Ray-Ban Display goes international

The $799 display glasses are now on sale in the UK (£749) and Canada ($1,149 CAD), with EU pre-orders open at €899 and shipping 13 October.

The camera-free Audio model is the interesting one. Privacy complaints have followed camera glasses since the first Ray-Ban Stories, and a version without one removes the main objection in offices, schools, and anywhere else cameras are unwelcome.

Where this leaves VR pricing

$1,300 sits just above Valve's Steam Frame at $1,059. The two are aimed at different people: Steam Frame is a PC gaming headset, while Meta is selling lightness, media, and passthrough, and asking buyers to wait six months or more for it.

Meta also teased Muse Charm, a keychain-sized device for its Muse AI assistant, with details promised later this year. And an FDA-cleared hearing-enhancement feature arrives in the US this autumn for a $149 one-time fee.

Our read

Meta has shown where it thinks headsets are going, and priced and dated it for 2027. What you can buy this autumn is glasses, not headsets. For most people, the Ray-Ban Gen 3 at $449 is the real Connect launch.
