Marvel's Wolverine is ten days out, and it's not the game people are expecting

Insomniac's Wolverine lands 15 September, PS5 exclusive. What's confirmed, and why it's darker than the Spider-Man comparison suggests.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

Marvel's Wolverine releases on 15 September, exclusively on PS5, from Insomniac Games — the studio behind Marvel's Spider-Man. That last fact is doing a lot of work in people's expectations, and it's worth correcting before launch, because this is not Spider-Man with claws.

What's confirmed

The basics

15 September 2026, PS5 exclusive, developed by Insomniac and published by Sony. There is no PC version announced and, unusually for a big PlayStation title, no cross-gen PS4 release.

Insomniac has described it as a single-player, narrative-driven, M-rated action game — brutal and violent, following Logan. The story picks up with Wolverine rejoining Team X after a three-year absence, against a backdrop of mutants being abducted. Sony has leaned on the violence in its own marketing, which tells you the studio isn't aiming for the broad, family-adjacent tone of its Spider-Man games.

Why it isn't "Spider-Man with claws"

The Spider-Man games are built around traversal — the swinging is the game, and the open city is the playground. Everything reported and shown about Wolverine points the other way: a more focused, combat-led experience rather than a sprawling open world defined by getting around it.

That's a deliberate design choice, and a sensible one. Wolverine doesn't swing. His appeal is close, savage combat and a healing factor that lets him take punishment no other Marvel lead can. A game built around that is necessarily tighter and more violent than a city-traversal sandbox. Expecting Spider-Man's structure is setting yourself up to misread what Insomniac is making.

What's not confirmed

Insomniac has kept the exact structure — how open it is, mission design, length — closer to the chest than it did with Spider-Man. Precise details on all of that are still thin, and anyone stating them firmly is extrapolating from trailers. We'll know properly at launch.

Where it sits in the calendar

Wolverine is the first genuinely major release in the autumn run-up to Grand Theft Auto VI, and it has 15 September largely to itself. For PS5 owners it's the standout exclusive of the season — and, as a single-player story with a definite ending, exactly the kind of game you can finish before GTA 6 arrives on 19 November.

The one real downside is the exclusivity. As a PS5-only, single-player title with no PC port announced, a large chunk of players simply can't play it, and there's no indication that changes soon. That's Sony's pattern with its biggest exclusives, and PC players have learned to expect a wait measured in years, if it comes at all.

Update, 14 September

The game is here and the reviews are in. Insomniac hit its 60fps-with-ray-tracing target on base PS5, and critics have praised the brutal combat and Liam McIntyre's Logan. Full launch detail in the launch and review post.
