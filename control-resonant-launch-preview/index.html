Control Resonant launches tomorrow, and Remedy has changed more than the main character

Remedy's Control sequel arrives 24 September on PS5, Xbox, and PC at $59.99, with Dylan Faden in the lead and a shift toward action-RPG. What's confirmed.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

Control Resonant releases tomorrow, 24 September, and it isn't simply Control 2. Remedy has swapped its lead character, moved the setting out of the Oldest House, and pushed the combat toward action-RPG. It's the last major release of a crowded September before October's run toward GTA 6.

What's confirmed

The release

24 September 2026 on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, and PC, priced at $59.99. A Mac version follows later in 2026. Developed and published by Remedy Entertainment.

That $59.99 price is worth noticing. With big releases this year sitting at $69.99 and up — Wolverine launched at $69.99 — Remedy has priced its sequel $10 under the going rate for a major release.

What's different

The biggest change is who you play as. Jesse Faden, the protagonist of the original, is missing. You play Dylan Faden, her brother, who was a supporting character in the first game. The Federal Bureau of Control releases him to deal with an extradimensional threat, and he goes looking for his sister along the way.

The setting moves too. Instead of the shifting Brutalist interiors of the Oldest House, Resonant takes place in a warped version of Manhattan, with gravity anomalies and familiar threats from the first game — the Hiss and the Mold — spreading through the city.

And the combat leans harder into action-RPG territory than the original, which was a third-person shooter with telekinetic powers layered on top. Remedy has framed it as a significant shift for the series.

Why it matters

Remedy has had a strong run — Alan Wake 2 was one of the best-received games of its year — and the original Control built a devoted audience on the strength of its atmosphere and strange, specific world. A sequel that changes the protagonist, the setting, and the combat at once is a genuine gamble. It could broaden the series, or it could lose what made the first game distinct.

Reviews will tell us which. We'll follow up once the verdict is in, the same way we did for Wolverine — where the gap between expectation and reception turned out to be the real story.
