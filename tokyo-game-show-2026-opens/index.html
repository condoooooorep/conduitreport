Tokyo Game Show opens today — the biggest ever, and where the real reveals will happen

TGS 2026 is the first five-day show in its 30-year history. Here's what's confirmed, when the Xbox broadcast airs, and where to watch the announcements land.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

Tokyo Game Show opens today, and it's the biggest in the event's history. For its 30th anniversary, TGS has expanded to a five-day format for the first time ever, under the theme "The Longest Five Days of Nonstop Play." Here's what's confirmed, when the broadcasts happen, and — the useful part — where the actual news will come from over the next few days.

What's confirmed

The show

TGS 2026 runs 17-21 September at Makuhari Messe, Chiba. Business days (trade only) are the 17th and 18th; public days are the 19th-21st, the first time TGS has run three public days. 759 exhibitors across nearly 4,000 booths from 51 countries, with roughly 300,000 visitors expected.

As always, Nintendo doesn't attend TGS — that's traditional, not a snub, so don't expect Switch 2 first-party news here. The show is dominated by Japanese publishers (Capcom, Square Enix, Sega, Bandai Namco) plus Xbox, PlayStation's partners, and a large mobile and indie presence.

The broadcasts that matter

The show floor is trade-only today, so the news comes from the streamed showcases, not the floor:

Xbox broadcast

The Xbox Tokyo Game Show 2026 stream airs today, 17 September, at 7:00 PM JST (3:00 AM PT / 6:00 AM ET), running roughly 50 minutes. Microsoft says it includes TGS-exclusive material, new gameplay, and games coming to Xbox. Minecraft Dungeons II and Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 are confirmed on the show floor.

Capcom Spotlight

Capcom's 40-minute showcase already aired on 16 September, a day ahead of the floor opening. Capcom is bringing six titles to TGS: Monster Hunter Wilds: Ascendance, Dragon's Dogma II: Dark Arisen, Mega Man: Dual Override, Pragmata, Onimusha: Way of the Sword, and Street Fighter 6.

Square Enix

A playable Final Fantasy VII Revelation demo runs throughout the event — the first hands-on with the remake trilogy's finale, which is dated 8 April 2027.

The thing worth knowing: at TGS, individual publishers run their own broadcasts, so the most interesting announcements often happen outside the main stream, scattered across the five days. This isn't a single-keynote show like a Nintendo Direct — it unfolds.

What to actually watch for

Three things, over the coming days rather than today:

Japanese heavyweights with something to show. Square Enix's FF7 Revelation demo is the headline, but watch for Sega, Bandai Namco, and Atlus (Persona 6 was recently teased) to use their booths for new footage.

Xbox's intent in Japan. Microsoft has spent years trying to win Japanese players and publishers. What it shows today — and whether it's genuinely TGS-exclusive or recycled — signals how serious that push still is.

The indie floor. TGS's "Selected Indie 80" program gives 80 indie developers free booths, and TGS has a track record of surfacing games that break out months later. The big reveals get headlines; the indie hall is where the surprises live.

The honest timing note

Today is opening day and trade-only, so the reveals are happening as this publishes rather than being fully reported yet. We'll follow this with a proper recap of what TGS actually revealed once the announcements have landed and been confirmed — the reveals deserve confirmed detail, not opening-hour guesses.
