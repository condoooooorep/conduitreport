Meta's Muse Spark Open is a free AI model you can run on two Mac Studios. Here's the catch

Meta released Muse Spark Open, an 816-billion-parameter open-weight model for coding and agents that runs on two Mac Studios. What it is and what it needs.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

Meta has released a new open-weight AI model, Muse Spark Open, and it's aimed squarely at the work every lab is chasing this autumn: coding and AI agents. Its headline claim is that a model this large can run on a desk rather than in a data centre.

Everything below is from Meta's announcement on 15 October, unless marked otherwise.

What's confirmed

The model

816 billion parameters, of which 42 billion are active for any given task, with a context of up to 1 million tokens. It handles text and images.

What it's for

Coding and agentic work: long tasks, complex software engineering, and multi-step reasoning.

Where it runs

Meta calls it "the first model in its class to run on your desktop using two Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studios," each with up to 512GB of memory.

Where to get it

The weights are downloadable from Hugging Face and the major cloud platforms, and several companies offer it as a paid API.

The licence

It's released under Meta's own Muse Model License, which Meta says has "limited commercial terms." Meta also says it gave the model extra safety training because anyone can run and modify open weights.

What "open" means here

Open-weight means you can download the model itself and run it on your own hardware, with no company in the middle reading your data or switching it off. That's the big difference from the models behind ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude, which you can't download and run yourself.

It isn't fully open-source in the strict sense: Meta's own licence limits some commercial use. If you're a business thinking of building on it, read the licence first.

The catch: "your desktop" is a big desk

Running it locally needs at least 1TB of memory, according to TechCrunch, citing Engadget. In practice that's two top-specification Mac Studios working together. That's far cheaper than a data centre, and far beyond a normal home computer.

For most people, the realistic way to use it is through one of the hosted APIs. Its real audience is developers and companies that want a capable model they control themselves.

How good is it

Meta says it's the most capable open-weight model from a US company, and that it outperforms other popular open-weight models on its own evaluations. Those are Meta's claims, on Meta's chosen tests. Independent results will follow now that anyone can run it, which is the point of releasing the weights.

Why it matters

It arrives in the same month that OpenAI and Anthropic have both disclosed their agents acting in unintended ways during testing, and that the FTC has stepped up its investigation into agent risks. A powerful open-weight model built for agent work cuts both ways: anyone can study and test it, and no company can pull it back once it's out. That's why Meta's extra safety training is part of the announcement, not a footnote.
