Marvel's Wolverine is out tomorrow, the reviews are in, and Insomniac stuck the landing

Wolverine launches 15 September, hits 60fps with ray tracing on base PS5, and reviews praise its brutal combat. What is confirmed at launch.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

Marvel's Wolverine launches tomorrow, 15 September, and unlike a lot of big releases this year, it arrives with reviews already out and largely positive. When we previewed it earlier this month, the open question was whether Insomniac could make a tighter, more violent game than its Spider-Man template. The early verdict is: yes.

What's confirmed at launch

The release

15 September 2026, PS5 exclusive, from Insomniac Games. Standard Edition $69.99; Digital Deluxe $79.99 (adds five exclusive suits). No early access — both editions unlock at the same time. Some regions cross into launch as early as 14 September by local timezone. No PC version announced.

The technical side

Insomniac says the game targets — and hits — 60fps with ray tracing on the base PS5, a target the studio says it aimed for from the start. Install size is around 81GB; preloading has been open since 8 September.

That 60fps-with-ray-tracing-on-base-PS5 claim is worth pausing on. Ray tracing usually forces a choice between visual quality and frame rate, and hitting both on the standard console (not just the Pro) is a genuine technical achievement — the kind of thing Insomniac has built its reputation on.

What the reviews say

Critical reception

Early reviews praise the brutal, aggressive combat, Insomniac's take on Logan, and Liam McIntyre's voice performance. The consensus frames it as a more focused, violent experience than the Spider-Man games rather than a copy of them — which is exactly what the studio said it was going for.

The combat is the standout in nearly every review — fast, savage, and built around Wolverine's healing factor letting you take punishment no other Marvel lead could. The story follows Logan rejoining Team X after a three-year absence, with mutants being abducted, and the supporting cast draws on the wider X-Men mythos, including Jean Grey.

The Reported stamp here is deliberate: these are critics' verdicts, not facts, and review consensus is a useful signal but not the same as a confirmed quality guarantee. Your mileage will vary with taste.

The one real catch

It's PS5-only, single-player, with no PC port announced. That's the same limitation we flagged in the preview, and launch hasn't changed it. Millions of players simply can't play this without buying Sony hardware, and Sony's pattern with its biggest exclusives is a PC port measured in years, if it comes at all.

For a single-player story with a definite ending, that's frustrating but not fatal — it's exactly the kind of game you can finish in a couple of weekends before GTA 6 takes over in November. But if you're on PC or Xbox, this one isn't for you, and there's no timeline that says otherwise.

Where it sits

Wolverine is the first genuinely major release in the autumn run-up to GTA 6, and it's landed well. If the reviews hold and the 60fps claim survives contact with players' actual consoles, Insomniac has delivered the strongest PS5 exclusive of the season. Update, 15 September

The game is now out and the full review wave has settled at a 78 Metacritic — favourable but below Insomniac's Spider-Man games, with combat praised and linearity/pacing the main knocks. Our launch-day verdict has the complete picture.
