How to spot a fake GTA 6 leak

The tells that give away fabricated claims, and the one rule that handles almost all of it.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

Between now and 19 November this is going to get worse. It's already bad, and the incentives are only sharpening — attention on this game converts into money at a rate that makes inventing things a viable business model.

So here's how to evaluate claims yourself, without depending on anyone to do it for you. Including this site.

Look at the source before the claim

The instinct when you see something is to ask whether it sounds plausible.

That's backwards, and fabricators know it. Plausibility is the easy part of the job. It's the first thing they get right.

Ask instead: who said this first, and what happens to them if it's wrong?

Rockstar's newswire, Take-Two's filings, first-party storefronts — these carry consequences for being wrong that run from embarrassment to securities liability. Reporters with a track record on this beat have reputations built over years that a single fabrication would end.

Anonymous accounts have none of that. An account wrong ten times and right once is remembered for the once. There's no cost to guessing, so assume guessing.

The tells

Some patterns recur often enough to be genuinely diagnostic.

No original source, ever. Follow it back. A remarkable number of viral claims terminate in a site citing a site citing a site, with nothing at the bottom. If nobody can point at where it started, it usually started with whoever's repeating it.

A screenshot instead of a link. Real announcements have URLs. A screenshot of a storefront listing, an internal email, or a deleted post is unfalsifiable by design and takes about forty seconds to fake in any browser's developer tools.

Specificity without verifiability. Fabrications love precise numbers — a file size, a build number, an exact map area — because specificity reads as authority. Real leaks are often vaguer, because real sources are protecting themselves.

It's exactly what everyone wants. PC at launch. A beloved character returning. A map bigger than anyone thought. That isn't evidence a claim is false. It's evidence the claim would have spread whether or not it were true, which is a different thing and a much weaker signal than it feels like.

Urgency framing. "Deleted within minutes." "Before this gets taken down." "Insider risking their job." Engagement technique, not corroboration.

Someone noticed a change. A page was edited, a timer exists, an asset was uploaded. Companies update pages constantly, for boring reasons, all day long.

The hard case

The genuinely difficult category isn't fabrication. It's material that's authentic but doesn't mean what people say it means.

Real development footage has circulated from this game more than once. Where the footage is real, the trap moves from forgery to interpretation — and interpretation is where nearly all the error actually lives.

Footage from an unfinished build shows a moment in a process. Features get cut. Placeholder assets get replaced. Debug tools get mistaken for player-facing systems. A menu in a development build may never ship and may never have been intended to.

That's why authentic-but-unofficial material still sits under a Rumour stamp here. Whether a video is real and whether the claims built on top of it are true are two separate questions, and people routinely answer the first and assume they've answered the second.

Six questions

Before believing or sharing anything:

  1. Can I find the original source, or only sites citing each other?
  2. Is there a URL on a Rockstar, Take-Two, Sony or Microsoft property?
  3. Does the person making the claim have a track record I can check?
  4. If this is wrong, does anything happen to them?
  5. Is this something I want to be true?
  6. If it's from a leak — is the claim about what the footage shows, or someone's reading of it?

Two or more failures and it belongs in the rumour column, however good it sounds.

The one rule

Rockstar Games announces Grand Theft Auto VI news on channels Rockstar Games controls.

The title. Both trailers. Both delays. Pre-orders, editions, pricing, the Netflix partnership. Every significant thing about this game for four years has been announced by the company, in writing, on its own newswire.

There's no reason the next one will be different, and that single rule will carry you through most of the eleven weeks of noise ahead.
