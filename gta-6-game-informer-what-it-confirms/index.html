GTA 6's Game Informer cover story: twice GTA V's map, hurricanes, and 170 species

Game Informer's GTA 6 cover story: a map about twice GTA V's, hurricanes, 170+ animal species and a new Heat system. What's confirmed and what's reported.

Carried by established outlets, not officially confirmed.

Game Informer's Grand Theft Auto VI cover story went live on 29 September: 14 pages built on interviews with Rockstar's senior developers, and 12 new screenshots. It's the most detail Rockstar has given about how Leonida works since the Extended Look in August.

Some of it is on Game Informer's free pages, and some sits behind its subscription. We've split the two. What Game Informer published openly carries a Confirmed stamp. What comes from the paywalled feature, as reported consistently by several outlets, carries a Reported stamp. That's why this post is stamped Reported overall.

The map

About twice GTA V

Rockstar's Aaron Garbut, head of development and co-studio head, told Game Informer that Leonida is roughly twice the size of GTA V's map and the biggest Rockstar has built "by some degree." He described it as denser and more detailed, with far more interiors: "We obsessed about every part – the rooftops, the alleys, the internals and everything in between."

That matches what Rockstar North's Rob Nelson told press in August: about twice GTA V, and three times Red Dead Redemption 2. Garbut's emphasis on interiors is the new part, and it's the answer to the question we've been asking since our map explainer: not how big it is, but how much of it you can walk into.

Six regions

Vice City, the Leonida Keys, Grassrivers, Port Gellhorn, Ambrosia, and Mount Kalaga National Park.

Weather

Storms and hurricanes

Game Informer confirms tropical storms and seasonal hurricanes. Weather is localised, so you can be in sunshine and watch rain fall across the map. Puddles and wet surfaces stay after rain passes, and rainbows appear in the right light.

Rebuilt from Red Dead

Owen Shepherd, Rockstar's vice president of art for lighting and rendering, said the studio "re-engineered our weather system to make everything much more localized and persistent."

What nobody knows yet is how often hurricanes happen, or whether they affect missions and the story. Game Informer says so itself.

Wildlife

170+ species

Across land, air, and sea, including flamingos, manatees, pelicans, and alligators, plus invasive species like green iguanas, cane toads, feral cats, and rhesus macaques. Some have never appeared in a Rockstar game. Certain animals are classed as Rare, Discoverable, or Legendary, a system that echoes Red Dead Redemption 2.

Outlets reporting from the full feature say that includes more than 60 kinds of bird and more than 30 land mammals.

Police, and what you'll do between crimes

The Heat system

Six-star wanted levels return, alongside a new Heat mechanic. Police identify you by your appearance and where a crime happened, so changing clothes or vehicles helps you get away.

Activities

Base jumping, billiards, gyms, kayaking, mini golf, scuba diving, street and off-road racing, zoo visits, airboats, and jet skis.

Game Informer's free pages also note that NPCs have more varied body types and more distinct features than in past Rockstar games.

What this changes

Two things. The map question now has a consistent answer from two senior Rockstar figures: about twice GTA V, and much denser. And the systems Rockstar chose to talk about, weather, wildlife, and police, are about the world rather than the plot. That's how the whole campaign has run: show the place, say little about the story.
