Apple's 9 September event: what's confirmed, and what's just very well sourced

Ternus's first keynote as CEO lands on 9 September. The date is confirmed. The foldable, the missing iPhone 18, the $2,000 price — none of it is, yet.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

Apple's next event is confirmed for Wednesday 9 September, 10 a.m. Pacific, at the Steve Jobs Theater. The invite went out on 26 August with the tagline "Surprise and shine."

That's the confirmed part, and it's worth separating cleanly from everything else, because the amount of detail circulating about what Apple will announce is enormous and almost none of it comes from Apple.

There's also a reason this particular event matters beyond the phones: it's John Ternus's first keynote as CEO. He took over from Tim Cook yesterday. So the first thing anyone sees of the post-Cook era is how he opens this show.

What's actually confirmed

Two things. The date, and the venue.

The event

Wednesday 9 September 2026, 10 a.m. PT, Steve Jobs Theater. Announced by Apple via its own invitation on 26 August.

Everything past that — every product, price, and spec below — is reporting and rumour. Apple has announced a date and nothing else. This is normal; Apple never previews its own events. But it means the confident product lists you're seeing elsewhere are sourced to supply-chain leaks and reporters, not to Apple.

What's expected — reported, not confirmed

The reporting here is unusually consistent, which raises confidence but doesn't make it official.

The lineup

Multiple established outlets expect the iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone 18 Pro Max, and Apple's first foldable iPhone — widely called the "iPhone Ultra." The standard iPhone 18, an 18e, and an Air 2 are reported to be held back to spring 2027, splitting Apple's annual launch into two for the first time.

That split is the genuinely new part of the story. For years Apple launched its whole iPhone line in September. If the reporting holds, this year you get only the expensive models in autumn and the affordable ones six months later. That's a significant change to how Apple sells its most important product, and it's worth watching whether Ternus addresses the logic of it directly.

The foldable is the headline. Reported as a book-style fold with a titanium frame, a near-crease-free inner display around 7.6–7.8 inches, and possibly Touch ID in the power button rather than Face ID. It would be Apple's first genuinely new iPhone form factor since the iPhone X in 2017.

The $2,000 price

Most estimates put the foldable near $1,999, with some suggesting higher configurations could approach $2,500. No price is official, and Apple has a history of confounding pre-event price predictions in both directions.

Other reported details: an A20 Pro chip on a 2nm process, a variable-aperture main camera on the Pro models, a smaller Dynamic Island, and Apple's own C2 cellular modem. Price rises are expected across the board, attributed to memory shortages — but "expected" is the operative word.

What's notably absent

No standard iPhone 18 in September, if the split reporting is right. No Apple Watch SE refresh, per one outlet. Macs and iPads — an M6 MacBook Pro, an OLED iPad mini, new Mac mini and Studio — are considered unlikely at this event and more probable at a later one, though nothing's confirmed either way.

What to actually watch on the 9th

Three things, in order of how much they'll tell you.

The foldable, obviously — whether it exists, what it costs, and whether it feels like a finished product or a first attempt. Apple entering a category years after Samsung is a bet that it can do it properly rather than first.

The launch split — whether Apple confirms holding the cheaper models to spring, and how it frames that. It changes the shape of the iPhone business.

And Ternus himself. Not the products — the tone. A first keynote is a statement of what a CEO thinks the company is. Cook's Apple was operations and services. Ternus is a hardware engineer taking over as the industry pivots to AI, and how he opens his first show is the first real signal of which way he'll steer.

We'll cover what's actually announced on the day. Until then: the date is real, the foldable is likely, and every price you've seen is a guess.

Update, 9 September

The event has happened. The foldable is real but is called iPhone Duo, not "Ultra," and starts at $1,999 — the rumoured price was right. The split launch was confirmed: only Pro models and the Duo appeared, no standard iPhone 18. Full confirmed detail in the event breakdown.
