The Witcher 3 Remastered: what's new, and why it scored 94

The Witcher 3 Remastered is free for owners and scored 94 on Metacritic, above the original's 92. The full list of changes, what critics said, and the caveat.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

An eleven-year-old game is now the best-reviewed release of 2026. The Witcher 3 Remastered landed on 29 September as a free update, and it's scored 94 on Metacritic. The original scored 92 in 2015.

There's one caveat worth knowing before quoting that number, and we'll get to it.

What's confirmed

The score

Metacritic 94, tied on score with the twin-stick shooter Sektori at the top of 2026's rankings, and ahead of this year's big new releases such as Resident Evil Requiem. The original Witcher 3 scored 92.

Players agree

On Steam, it's rated Very Positive, with 92% of more than 6,000 recent reviews recommending it.

What it is

A free update for existing owners on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, PC, and Switch 2, with both expansions included. Existing saves and achievements carry over. CD Projekt co-CEO Michał Nowakowski said charging current owners "was never a question we asked ourselves." On Switch 2 it's a separate eShop product: digital owners of the Switch version get it automatically, and physical owners can claim a free upgrade.

Download size

Around 45GB on PC according to PC Gamer, and around 50GB on Switch 2.

What's new

The skill system

Skills are now arranged in a tree, each with three ranks, and unlocking one requires its prerequisites.

Visuals

Native DirectX 12 with multithreading, ray-traced HairWorks, textures from the fan-made HD Reworked Project, improved skin rendering, and better lighting on grass and foliage.

Reforging and photo mode

Master craftsmen can now change the look of your gear without changing its stats, which is transmog in all but name. Photo mode lets you place and pose characters, add lights and props, and control weather and time of day.

There's also a new accessibility preset, "Bard's Ballad," with infinite durability, unlimited breath, and a toggle for Witcher Senses, plus a Ukrainian localisation.

The caveat

The 94 on PC came from only nine reviews when GamesRadar+ reported it. The original game's PC score was built on 79. Metacritic scores built on a handful of reviews can move as more come in, so treat "best of the year" as where it stands today, not a final result.

What critics praised

The consensus is that it's now the definitive version. GamingBolt gave it a 9 and called it "the definitive way to play The Witcher 3." Push Square gave it a 10, calling it "still an RPG masterpiece," and singled out movement: Geralt is "far more responsive when it comes to turning, sprinting, and stopping." Press Start called the Switch 2 version "a fantastic achievement."

What didn't change

Push Square, for all its praise, said the remaster doesn't bring "truly game-changing additions," and listed the old problems that remain: combat is still clunky in fights against several enemies, the new transmog feature unlocks late, and autosaves still cause brief hitches. On a base PS5, ray tracing runs at 30fps, which Push Square called a "questionable trade off."

Should you play it

Yes, if you've never played The Witcher 3. This is now the version to start with, and it costs the same as the game did before.

Worth a return, if you finished it years ago. The reworked skill tree and better movement change how it plays, but the story and quests are the same, so it's a replay, not a new game.
