Apple's event is tomorrow: how to watch, and the three things that actually matter

The "Surprise and Shine" keynote is 9 September, 10am Pacific. Start times worldwide, where to watch, and what to watch for beyond the phones.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

Apple's "Surprise and Shine" keynote is tomorrow — Wednesday 9 September, 10:00 a.m. Pacific. It's the first iPhone event of the post-Tim-Cook era, and reportedly the start of the biggest run of new Apple hardware in the company's history. This is the short version: when it's on where you are, where to watch, and the three things worth actually watching for.

For the full breakdown of what's rumoured — the foldable's specs, the pricing estimates, the split launch — see our preview from last week. This piece is about the event itself.

The three things that actually matter

Not the spec sheet — the trailers will drown you in that. These are the questions that make tomorrow worth watching rather than just reading about afterwards.

1. The foldable, if it's real. Apple entering a category Samsung has owned for years is its first genuinely new iPhone form factor since 2017. The question isn't whether it's powerful — it's whether Apple did the fold properly: the crease, the hinge, the software. If it looks like a finished product rather than a first attempt, that's the story of the day. If it's priced near $2,000, watch how Apple justifies it.

2. The launch split. Reporting says only the expensive models come tomorrow — Pro, Pro Max, foldable — with the regular iPhone 18, the 18e, and the Air 2 held to spring 2027. If Apple confirms that, it's a real change to how it sells its most important product: no affordable new iPhone until spring. Watch whether Apple frames the split openly or just quietly doesn't mention a base model.

3. Ternus. This is his first keynote as CEO. The products were locked long before he took over, so they won't tell you much about him — but the tone will. Cook's Apple was operations and services. Ternus is a hardware engineer taking the chair as the industry pivots hard to AI. How he opens, what he emphasises, whether Apple says anything real about AI after a year of being seen as behind — that's the first genuine signal of where he steers the company.

What to do with it

Same advice as any big keynote: watch it once, live or on replay, before you read the frame-by-frame breakdowns. Apple events are heavily staged, and the reaction economy will have hot takes up before the stream even ends. The announcements only get to be new once.

Then come back and separate the three piles, the way this site always does: what Apple confirmed on stage, what gets reported in the days after as details leak out of hands-on areas, and what stays rumour until it ships. Tomorrow, almost everything moves from rumour to confirmed in ninety minutes — which is exactly why it's worth watching.

We'll have the breakdown of what Apple actually announced once the keynote's done.

The keynote is over. On the three things to watch: the foldable (iPhone Duo, $1,999) is real and Ternus led with it; the split launch was confirmed (Pro and Duo only, no base iPhone 18); and Ternus's first keynote leaned hardware-first. The full breakdown has every confirmed price and date.
