GTA 6: An Extended Look — when it starts where you are, and what Rockstar has actually promised

Confirmed start times for every major region, and a clear line between what Rockstar has actually promised and what fans are hoping for.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

Fifteen months. That's how long it's been since Rockstar showed anyone a frame of Grand Theft Auto VI. Trailer 2 landed in May 2025, half a billion people watched it inside a day, and then the studio went quiet in the way it always does — no drip-feed, no developer diaries, nothing.

That ends this afternoon. Grand Theft Auto VI: An Extended Look premieres on Netflix on Thursday 27 August, and arrives free on Rockstar's YouTube channel and the official site six hours after that.

Both of those facts come from Rockstar's own newswire and from Netflix. Almost everything else you've read about today does not, and the back half of this piece is about telling those two piles apart before you rearrange your evening around one of them.

When it starts

Two moments. Netflix at 3:00 p.m. Eastern, free release at 9:00 p.m. Eastern, both on the 27th. Netflix subscribers get a six-hour head start; after that the subscription stops mattering.

If you're east of Greenwich, the short version is that the premiere lands somewhere in the small hours and the free one lands around breakfast. Sleeping through the first costs you nothing except the ability to be first in a group chat, which I accept is not nothing.

On Netflix it's a normal title page, so searching the name finds it. It isn't tucked away in the games section.

What Rockstar has actually said about it

This is where a lot of this week's coverage quietly stopped reporting and started wishing.

Rockstar's post calls it an extended look at Grand Theft Auto VI, the next evolution in the series. Netflix's announcement adds the one genuinely useful technical detail: the footage was captured entirely in-game on PlayStation 5.

That phrasing is worth pausing on, because Rockstar has used it before and it isn't standard industry language. When Trailer 2 arrived, the studio went out of its way to say it was gameplay and cutscenes captured in-game on PS5 rather than pre-rendered. Nobody made them say that. It's a claim that would be embarrassing to walk back, and they've now made it twice.

What Rockstar has not published: a runtime, a format, whether anyone speaks over it, or any hint that the release date features in it.

The twenty-to-thirty-minute runtime

This figure has been repeated across dozens of sites this month with no official source behind any of them. It might be right. It's still one unsourced number wearing a lot of confidence.

"Extended look" is not "trailer," and Rockstar knows it

Half the headlines this month called this Trailer 3. Rockstar didn't.

They named the first two things Trailer 1 and Trailer 2. They named this one an extended look. A studio that careful with language is being careful on purpose, and the distinction shapes what you should reasonably expect.

A trailer is cut for feeling. An extended look, in the vocabulary the industry has settled into, usually means something longer and more explanatory — the kind of thing where a mechanic gets demonstrated rather than glimpsed for four frames.

There's a supporting argument in the shape of the campaign so far. Trailer 1 gave us the place and Lucia. Trailer 2 gave us Jason, the wider cast, and a much bigger view of Leonida. Neither showed a HUD, a mission structure, a wanted level, or a menu. At some point before people hand over eighty dollars, the studio has to show them the thing they'll actually be operating, and there are twelve weeks left in which to do it.

That's reasoning from Rockstar's own past behaviour, not information. If this turns out to be a straight cinematic, my reasoning was wrong — the sources weren't.

The Netflix deal is the genuinely odd part

Sit with how strange it is for a second.

Rockstar has never had a reach problem. Trailer 2 was watched around half a billion times in twenty-four hours on channels the company already owns. Whatever this partnership solves, it isn't distribution.

Two readings, both speculative. The first is that Netflix is buying cultural relevance in gaming, which it has been chasing for years with patchy results, and has paid for a moment rather than a product.

The second one interests me more: Rockstar may want this watched on televisions. If the footage is long, dense and technically ambitious, the gap between a compressed vertical clip and a living-room screen is enormous, and a Netflix premiere shoves an awful lot of people onto the second one.

Neither company has explained the deal. Take both as reasoning.

What today doesn't change

The date. 19 November 2026, PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. It's on Rockstar's site, Sony's product page, and in Take-Two's investor materials. A marketing beat doesn't move a date in either direction.

The platforms. PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, nothing else announced. If the Extended Look closes on a PC logo, that's real news. Until it does, a PC version is a reasonable expectation built on precedent, and precedent is not an announcement.

The editions. Standard at $79.99, Ultimate at $99.99, Vintage Vice City Pack on pre-orders. Nothing about today suggests movement there.

Watch it once without pausing

Unsolicited advice, take it or leave it.

The reflex with Rockstar footage is to freeze every third frame and start reading storefront signage. It's a real and enjoyable part of this hobby and there'll be a fortnight of breakdowns to read afterwards, including on this site.

But it only gets to be new once. Thirteen years of waiting, and the first proper look at it lasts however long it lasts. The analysis will still be there in an hour.

Then take it apart. And when you do, keep hold of the distinction this whole piece has been circling: something running on screen is strong evidence. Something inferred from two frames of a menu is not, however many upvotes the thread has. That gap is where essentially every piece of Grand Theft Auto VI misinformation of the last three years has come from, and it's about to get a lot wider.

Update, 28 August

The Extended Look ran 26 minutes, so the widely repeated runtime figure — flagged above as unsourced — turned out to be right. No PC version was announced, so that position is unchanged. And the prediction in this piece was half right: Rockstar did demonstrate mechanics rather than glimpse them, showing layered carjacking and protagonist switching, but a substantial share of the 26 minutes was cutscene and montage rather than controller-in-hand play.
