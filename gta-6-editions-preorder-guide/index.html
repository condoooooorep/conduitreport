Standard or Ultimate: what the extra twenty dollars actually buys

What Rockstar has confirmed is in each edition, what every pre-order gets, and the honest case for not deciding yet.

Stated by the maker, publisher, or platform holder.

Pre-orders opened at local midnight on 25 June. There are two editions and one bonus pack, which is a simpler situation than the internet has managed to make it.

Let's clear the biggest bit of noise first. There is no Collector's Edition. No Special Edition, no box with a statue, no steelbook tier. Rockstar built two SKUs. Any page offering you a third is either wrong or selling something that isn't the game.

The two editions

Standard, $79.99. The whole game. Full single-player campaign across Vice City and the wider state of Leonida, plus the online component. Nothing in the story is locked behind the higher tier — worth stating plainly, because that's the thing people are most anxious about.

Ultimate, $99.99. Everything above, plus a bundle of premium content that unlocks across Jason and Lucia's story rather than dumping into your inventory in hour one.

Regional prices are set by each storefront and have moved around by market, so check yours rather than converting from dollars.

What the extra twenty gets you

From Rockstar's own listings:

  • Vehicles — a '95 Grotti Cheetah, a Shitzu Squalo, a '67 Vapid Dominator Buggy, and a Vapid Ganado

modification kit

  • Weapons — personalised Hawk & Little Morgan revolvers
  • Apparel and tattoos for both protagonists
  • Four exclusive shops — Rideout Customs, Sara's Unisex Salon, Stock 305, Electric Fang Tattoo — which

Standard players don't get access to at all

  • A special commission to restore classic cars, which reads like an activity chain rather than a

cosmetic drop

The structural detail matters more than the shopping list. This content is threaded through the campaign and unlocks chapter by chapter. That's a considerably better design than most premium editions manage, where you get everything at the title screen and it trivialises the opening hours.

It also means you can't evaluate it on day one, which cuts both ways.

What everyone gets

Both editions come with the Vintage Vice City Pack:

  • A '55 Vapid Stanier and the Shore Court garage near Ocean Beach to keep it in. The garage has a

weapon locker and somewhere to fence stolen goods, so it's functional rather than ornamental

  • Vintage outfits and hairstyles — a pastel linen suit for Jason, a red sequin dress for Lucia
  • A tropical weapon pattern, explicitly a nod to Tommy Vercetti's shirt

Now the footnote nobody puts in the headline: this bonus isn't really a pre-order bonus. Rockstar attached it to digital purchases made before 20 November. Buy it on launch day and you still get it.

The free GTA+ month

Digital pre-orders through the PlayStation and Microsoft stores have been reported to include a month of GTA+. Check it's still attached when you're at the checkout rather than assuming.

Is Ultimate worth it?

No affiliate links here, so here's the actual argument rather than a recommendation dressed as one.

For. The four shops are the strongest part by a distance. Vehicle and character customisation are two of the systems people sink the most hours into across a Grand Theft Auto lifecycle. Four exclusive vendors is a persistent difference, not a novelty car you drive twice. If you know from experience that you're the sort of person who loses two hours in a customisation menu, twenty dollars for more menu is defensible.

Against. You're paying in advance, for content you can't see, in a game you haven't played, off a bullet list. The vehicles are cosmetic variants of cars that will have non-exclusive equivalents. The clothes are clothes. And both platforms let you buy the Ultimate upgrade later, which turns this from a decision into a decision you can postpone.

Neither, yet. Nothing about the bonus requires pre-ordering. Digital stock can't sell out. The Vintage Vice City Pack runs through 19 November. Unless you want a physical copy — where retail stock genuinely is finite — waiting costs you literally nothing and buys eleven weeks of information, including reviews.

Something worth knowing before you decide

Grand Theft Auto pre-order exclusives have not historically stayed exclusive. Content introduced as a special-edition incentive in past entries has found its way into general availability afterwards.

Nobody has said that will happen here and Rockstar may well handle it differently. But if the thing driving your $99.99 is a fear of permanently missing a car, that particular fear has a poor track record.

Where to buy without getting burned

Rockstar Store, PlayStation Store, Microsoft Store, and the established retailers in your country — Amazon, Best Buy, GameStop and their national equivalents. Prices are effectively identical; occasional retailer discounts exist and are small.

Avoid: key resellers well under $79.99, anything promising PC access, and anything mentioning early access. There is no PC version announced and there is no early access programme, and those two lies are the hooks in most of the current wave of scams.

PC pre-orders

Grey-market listings for a PC version are appearing. Rockstar has announced PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. There is nothing legitimate to pre-order on PC, because there is nothing to pre-order.

Standard gets you the whole game and the whole story. Ultimate adds cosmetics, cars and four shops spread across the campaign. Everyone buying before 20 November gets the Vintage pack. If you're going digital, September and November are the same decision — one of them just comes with more information.
